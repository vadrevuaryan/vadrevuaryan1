DWG 01 · Team project, 5 members

Open-Cycle Liquid Rocket Engine

A simplified gas-generator (open-cycle) liquid rocket engine, modeled and assembled in SolidWorks. A gas generator drives a turbine that spins the fuel and oxidizer turbopumps on a common shaft. The propellants meet at the injector plate, burn in the combustion chamber, and expand through a bell nozzle. The turbine exhaust is dumped overboard, which is what makes the cycle "open", the same layout used on the Saturn V's F-1.

Role
Combustion chamber, injector plate, nozzle
Tools
SolidWorks parts, assembly, drawings
Assembly
13 line items, 30+ part files
Course
CAD project, Rutgers
  • Modeled the combustion chamber, injector plate and nozzle for efficient propellant mixing and high-pressure containment.
  • Analyzed the turbopump and cooling layout using fluid dynamics and structural analysis principles.
  • Brought thermodynamics, combustion and fluid dynamics together into one system-level model.
  • Routed the feed lines between tanks, pumps, gas generator and chamber. Getting every pipe to mate cleanly was the hardest part of the assembly.

DWG 02 · Design of Mechanical Components · Spring 2026 · Team of 8

Economical Gimbal System

Handheld gimbals are either expensive or tied to one device. We designed a multi-axis camera and phone gimbal that can be 3D-printed in ABS with standard hardware. It went from rough sketches to SolidWorks, was redesigned in Fusion 360, and was finalized in SolidWorks with GD&T on the critical mating features.

  • Selected ABS for the printed structure on strength, weight, cost and printability.
  • Hand-calculated bending moments, stress concentrations and center of mass to check the structure under load.
  • Applied GD&T and tolerancing to the Ø18 mm pin and ¼-20 fastener interfaces so the parts fit and rotate as intended.
  • Designed for additive manufacturing so it scales without mold retooling, the main cost driver in molded gimbals.

DWG 03 · Multiphysics Simulations · Spring 2026 · Team of 3

CPU Heat Sink Fin Study

Which matters more for a CPU heat sink, fin shape or fin material? We tested both together in ANSYS. A 2D CFD model validated the convective heat transfer coefficient against published values. That coefficient then fed 3D steady-state thermal and flow simulations of four materials and three pin-fin geometries in a ducted airflow.

Tools
ANSYS Fluent, MATLAB
Materials
Cu, Al, leaded brass, 304 SS
Fins
Rectangular, circular, triangular
h
16.844 W/m²·K (2D CFD)
306.7 Klowest base temp: copper, rectangular fins
51.9 Kcooler than stainless steel, triangular fins
12material and geometry combinations
  • Ran a mesh convergence study on maximum temperature before trusting any results.
  • Found thermal conductivity was the dominant factor, and copper ran coolest with every fin shape.
  • Rectangular fins beat circular and triangular fins in every material by giving the air the most surface area.
  • Kept boundary conditions identical across all runs so materials and shapes could be compared directly.

DWG 04 · Mechatronics · Team of 5

Distance-Sensing Rotating Turret

A semi-autonomous turret that rotates to a commanded angle, checks for a target within 30 cm with an ultrasonic sensor, sounds a buzzer, and launches a BB. All six mechatronic subsystems are in it: display, audio output, manual input, automatic sensing, actuators, and logic control.

Controller
Arduino Mega (C++)
Sensing
Ultrasonic, 30 cm trigger
Actuation
2 servos, 2 DC roller motors
Output
LCD angle readout, buzzer
  • The base servo turns the launcher by the angle typed into the serial monitor, and the LCD shows it.
  • An arm servo drives a linkage that turns rotary motion into a linear push, feeding each BB into two spinning DC rollers.
  • Programmed the Arduino to read the sensor, decide whether to fire, and reset the mechanism automatically.
  • Used MATLAB to analyze and plot sensor readings and system performance.
